<SectionHeading id="what">What a tolerance profile sets</SectionHeading>

<Prose>

A <Term id="tolerance-profile">tolerance profile</Term> sets for each quantity how far a <Term id="value">value</Term> of the newer result may lie above and below the value of the older one before the difference counts as a <Term id="deviation">deviation</Term>. It is a file in JSON. You hand it with the option `--tolerance` to one of the commands {command('replay')}, {command('regression')}, {command('walk')} and {command('search')}.

There is no general tolerance and no profile that holds by itself. Without `--tolerance` each of these commands compares exactly, and a quantity that has no entry in the profile is compared exactly.

A tolerance is what a comparison deliberately does not see. So every rounding and every bound above zero comes with a derivation: a sentence, or a reference to a document, that says where the number comes from. The result of a comparison holds the profile with its derivations; whoever reads the result sees what was compared with.

</Prose>

<RowList>
  <Row name="The quantities" code="per_quantity">One entry per quantity that has a tolerance, under the name of the quantity. The list may be empty; then every quantity is compared exactly.</Row>
  <Row name="The upper bound" code="upper">How far the newer value may lie above the older one.</Row>
  <Row name="The lower bound" code="lower">How far the newer value may lie below the older one.</Row>
  <Row name="The absolute part of a bound" code="abs_tol">A number in the unit of the quantity, in the example `0.01` for one cent.</Row>
  <Row name="The relative part of a bound" code="rel_tol">A share of the larger of the two absolute values that are compared; `0.001` stands for one per mille.</Row>
  <Row name="The decimal places" code="decimals" tag="optional">An integer from 0 to 15. Both values are rounded to that many decimal places before they are compared.</Row>
  <Row name="The derivation" code="derivation">Where the decimal places and the bounds come from, as text or as a reference to a document. It is required as soon as the entry rounds or a part of a bound is above zero.</Row>
</RowList>

<Prose>

Every entry needs both bounds, and every bound both parts; a part that is not to hold is `0`. No part is negative. Numbers are written with a point as the decimal mark.

For `replay` and `regression` the older value is that of the recorded run, and the newer one that of the run the command computes. `walk` and `search` compare two states of the history; the older one is the earlier state.

</Prose>

<SectionHeading id="rule">When a value deviates</SectionHeading>

<Prose>

When the entry of a quantity has `decimals`, both values are rounded first, commercially: a 5 rounds away from zero. Then the comparison forms the difference “newer value minus older value”.

Each bound is the larger of its two parts: the absolute part, or the relative part times the larger of the two absolute values. The value deviates when the difference is greater than the upper bound, or when it lies further below zero than the lower bound.

</Prose>

<RowList>
  <Row name="Exactly at the bound is no deviation">A difference that is exactly as large as its bound does not count. With an upper bound of `0.25`, 100.25 does not deviate from 100.</Row>
  <Row name="The two bounds hold separately">With an upper bound of `0.25` and a lower bound of `0`, 100 deviates from 100.25: the newer value lies below the older one, and for that the lower bound holds. A profile with equal bounds compares alike in both directions.</Row>
  <Row name="The relative part grows with the value">With a relative part of `0.001`, 1201 does not deviate from 1200: the bound is 0.001 times 1201, that is 1.201.</Row>
  <Row name="Rounding does not hide a rounding step">With two decimal places, 0.6972 and 0.6974 both give 0.70 and do not deviate. 0.6949 and 0.6951 give 0.69 and 0.70 and deviate, although they lie closer together.</Row>
  <Row name="Round first, then compare exactly">For a quantity with defined decimal places the first choice is to round and to compare exactly: `decimals` with both bounds at zero. A bound above zero is for what remains after that.</Row>
  <Row name="Per quantity, and no finer">A tolerance holds for every value of its quantity. There is no tolerance per model point, per period or per tariff.</Row>
</RowList>

<SectionHeading id="faults">When a profile is refused</SectionHeading>

<Prose>

A command checks the profile before it computes. When it finds a fault, it ends with <Term id="exit-status">exit status</Term> 2, names the file and the fault and writes nothing.

</Prose>

<RowList>
  <Row name="The file does not exist">The message begins with `no tolerance file at`. Check the path after `--tolerance`.</Row>
  <Row name="The file is no valid JSON">The message holds `is not JSON` and names line and column. Often a quotation mark or a comma is missing, or there is a comma too many.</Row>
  <Row name="The form is not right">The message holds `violates its schema at` and names the place: a bound or a part is missing, a part is negative, or the derivation is missing although the entry rounds or a part is above zero.</Row>
  <Row name="A quantity is unknown">The message names the name and ends with `which the run does not declare`. Compare the name with the first line of `results.csv`.</Row>
</RowList>
